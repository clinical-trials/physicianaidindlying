The 14 authorizing US jurisdictions

Roughly a third of Americans now live where medical aid in dying is authorized. Every one of these requires a six-month terminal prognosis and self-administration; the meaningful variation is in who may prescribe, how long the clock runs, and whether a clinician who declines has to tell the patient anything.

2026 legislative pipeline

Seventeen states introduced aid-in-dying bills or amendments in the 2026 session. Assume one to two new authorizing jurisdictions per year through 2030, weighted toward the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic, with Virginia as the swing case that would open the South.

The international landscape

What legislators most often want and least often get: how comparable countries actually designed this, and what happened afterwards. Utilization ranges from Italy's 15 deaths in seven years to the Netherlands' 5.97% of all deaths — a difference driven far more by administration modality, workforce and procedural friction than by the breadth of the right.

What each policy lever actually does

Eight design choices account for most of the variation in access between jurisdictions. For each: the question a legislature has to answer, what the evidence from operating regimes shows, and what it implies. This is the part of the record that rarely reaches a committee hearing.

Reporting and compliance systems

Six jurisdictions operate purpose-built software for assisted-dying reporting. The United States, across all fourteen jurisdictions and twenty-eight years, uses PDF forms returned by email or fax.
